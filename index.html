What I'm doing now

When Inkbox's consumer business closed in late 2025, BIC kept six of us on to build in-house engineering capability. I'm now one of three engineers, alongside QA, on a new Global Supply Chain team building software for BIC's factories and supply chain company-wide.

In the quieter stretches I've done a lot of self-directed work: a family of open-source TypeScript libraries, and plenty of time directing AI coding agents through real architecture and maintenance work.

I'm open to new opportunities, whether a full-time role or a contract, where I can do meaningful work and keep growing.

How I work

I look for the pattern, not just the ticket. At Inkbox I turned a 1,489-line service into a strategy pattern, and turned a DTO convention into a shared package other codebases now use.

I'm good at finding things. When a teammate needs to know where a bug or feature actually lives, I can usually track it down. Then I like to simplify what I find into reusable pieces: DTOs, Laravel facades, shared helpers.

I let the evidence make the case. When there's a real disagreement about a design, I'd rather investigate or prototype the alternatives and compare results than argue. At Benevity, while building Forms' integration with the Grants API, I traced the business rules through and found the API didn't expose data Forms needed. Once I'd shown the gap, I was asked to take over and build the missing pieces myself.

I make pragmatic calls. I benchmarked a custom sorting algorithm against PHP's built-in usort under a 5-second budget. Mine came out slightly ahead but was still too slow for the budget, so I shipped the built-in.

I raise the floor for the team. At Benevity I helped introduce Jest to a Grants codebase that had no JavaScript test coverage, and took shifts owning the team's weekly release end to end. Earlier, I mentored junior developers at BID13.

I use AI tools critically. I direct coding agents through real work, and that includes reviewing their architecture and catching the bugs they introduce.

Selected work

Inventory & Distribution system (Inkbox, 2023)

I designed and built it end to end: data model, schema, Nova admin tooling and integration with the platform's DTO layer, covering tens of thousands of SKUs. Then I hardened it through a real production bug-fix cycle.

Sheet generation refactor (Inkbox, 2023–24)

A 1,489-line service became 128 lines, backed by a GeneratesSheets interface, six generator classes and shared traits. The new structure made it easy to extend later for bundled products, and to add caching.

The DTO pattern, from convention to package (Inkbox, 2021 onward)

I introduced the Data Transfer Object pattern early in the core app's life and maintained it for years. When other codebases needed it, I directed its extraction into a standalone shared package.

Grants Forms service (Benevity)

One of the projects I'm proudest of. I helped guide it and built much of it from the ground up, including a configurable adapter that routes form submissions to any receiving API.

What I build on my own time

I maintain a small ecosystem of TypeScript libraries that build on each other: a JSON-to-DOM rendering layer, a matrix/grid layer on top of it, a lightweight DOM implementation for testing, and the shared build and test tooling underneath them all. Battleship is the proof that the whole stack works.

  • Battleship: a multiplayer-ready game built on the whole stack. Play it · source
  • JSON DOM: a from-scratch virtual-DOM framework with a composable trait system (docs).
  • Matrix DOM: grid and matrix geometry helpers, used by Battleship's board.
  • Pseudo DOM: a lightweight DOM for testing in Node, on npm, and used to test this site.
  • Sí, funciona: dependency-light, fully typed utility functions.
  • Collect your stuff (and go)!: classic collection types, such as queues, stacks and linked lists.

  • Common Exports: converts ES Modules to CommonJS for dual-format builds.
  • js-build-tools: the shared build, test and docs pipeline behind all of the above.
  • Title Switcher: my typing-effect plugin, on npm.
